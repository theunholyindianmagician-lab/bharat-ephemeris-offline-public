१ · चार गणना-तह, आकाश के सामने · The four choices against the sky

The sky here is the Modern Bhāratīya (dṛk) choice, this engine's own series; it is served for the years its label names and refused outside them. Each text choice is compared in two frames: sidereal, where the sky's frame is Citrā-pakṣa, and tropical, where each choice adds its own ayanāṃśa — the frame-free, physical comparison. Minutes of arc; a positive sign means the choice is ahead of the sky.

२ · १०१′ का हिसाब · Where the text's Sun stands, and why

The reasons, from the text's own numbers

    The arcsecond agreement this repository measures belongs to the dṛk choice alone (its label states the figure and against what). A text choice is the ancestors' model at the ancestors' rates; its distance from the sky is the text's, and the paramparā's records narrow it where they speak.

    ३ · परम्परा के सूत्र, एक-एक करके लागू · The paramparā's records, applied one by one

    Each record of the registry that can be applied was applied where it belongs, and the result measured against the sky over 2026 (every 5 days) and over 1900–2100 (every 97 days) in the tropical frame. What improved was adopted; what did not is reported. The data is corpus/research/parampara-apply.json, written by scripts/parampara-apply.cjs; rows are candidate minus the sky, in minutes of arc (mean · rms · max).

    ५ · सूक्ष्म-काल · The resolution the vargas demand

    A varga D-n divides each sign into n parts. The finest of Parāśara's sixteen, the ṣaṣṭyāṃśa, is 30′; the nāḍī texts' D-150 is 12′. The lagna moves about a degree in four minutes of clock and the Moon 13° in a day, so every part is a span of birth time, and a placement is decided only when the body stands farther from the part's edge than the uncertainty of its longitude. The first table gives, at the instant and site above, what one part is worth in time, and how many parts each choice's measured uncertainty of the Moon spans. The second states each choice's placements of the lagna, the Sun and the Moon with the exact margin to the nearest edge (sukshma-kala.js, micro-arcsecond arithmetic) and whether the placement is decided under that uncertainty: for the sky choice its measured figures, for a text choice its measured distance from the sky (section 3). The lattice 27 × 4 = 108 = 12 × 9 links the nakṣatra loop to the graha loop; the Vimśottarī balance is the part of the nakṣatra gone, so one minute of arc of the Moon is days of daśā — the last line.

    कालचक्र-दशा on the 108 cells · Parāśara 46.52–46.100

    The Kālacakra daśā is the text's own use of the lattice: the Moon's cell (4 × nakṣatra + pāda) read mod 12 is the aṃśaka of 46.88, and each nakṣatra-pāda carries a chain of nine signs whose years (46.84, by the sign's lord) sum to the paramāyus of 46.89 — 100, 85, 83, 86 by the aṃśaka's trine. The sixteen chains come in reversed pairs; every step is one sign except at the three motions of 46.96–100. The table is the module's data with the text's checks (kalachakra.js); the lines after it give, for each choice's Moon at the instant above, the cell, the chain and the running sign with its balance by 46.93.       ७ · जो दावा नहीं है · What is not claimed

      • Accuracy of a text choice against today's sky beyond what section 1 shows for the day you pick: the text's Moon has one equation (SS 2.29-2.45), and no record in the registry corrects that; the elongation's scatter (section 3) is the text's.
      • Independence of the dṛk choice from JPL: its series was fitted to this engine's N-body, which is restarted from NASA-JPL DE440s states; its label says so.
      • Āryabhaṭa's epicycles and the Parahita planets: no local edition attests them, so the Kerala choice carries the text's epicycles and the text's five star-planets, labelled.
      • Any figure not on this page or in a test: the design document (private) tags every claim the same way.
